Help check the index of the Bonet dictionary

The index of the dictionary of Jean Bonet (1899) links every headword character to its place on the scan. It was made by a computer and is not always right. Each card shows one place of the scan with the character and the reading that the index found. If both are right, tap Correct or swipe the card to the right; if not, type the right reading or character and tap Save. How it works

The window shows one headword of the dictionary: the word in Quốc Ngữ, then its character, framed. Check both against "The index reads".

  • Correct (or swipe the card to the right, or the key Enter): the character and the reading in the fields are right. Where the index knows several readings, tap the one that is printed first.
  • Save: you changed the reading or the character. Type the reading as Bonet prints it, in lower case (Telex: aa → â, ow → ơ, s → sắc). The character field offers the characters of a reading; a character that Unicode does not have can be written as an IDS (e.g. ⿰亻尔).
  • Not a headword: the frame is not on the character of a headword (a Latin word, a rule, a character inside an example).
  • Swipe left, the arrow on the right or the key → goes on without an answer; the arrow on the left or the key ← goes back. Unsure? Just skip it.
Your answers are kept in this browser only. Export downloads them as a file; Import reads a file back (e.g. on another device).
