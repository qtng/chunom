A warning song against gambling, in the song thất lục bát metre (108 lines). It is spoken in the voice of the wives, who plead with the men who waste their days and money on cards and games, ruin their families and turn friends into enemies. 108 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Date: 1921
𧊉Bướm花Hoa新Tân傳Truyện Bướm Hoa Tân Truyện
Story of Butterflies and Flowers (woodblock version) · A verse tale in dialogue between a butterfly and a flower, in the woodblock edition reprinted in January 1916 by Quán Văn Đường. In the spring the butterfly courts the flower, who answers with doubts about the sincerity and the fate of such love; the two talk of longing, loyalty and the sorrows of the world. 404 lines transcribed by trungDate: reprint 01/1916
胡hồ春xuân香hương詩thi集tập Hồ Xuân Hương Thi Tập
A collection of short poems attributed to Hồ Xuân Hương, in 31 pages (poems), of which the transcription is still unfinished. The subjects are everyday objects and sights – a well, a fan, a sleeping woman, the moon, caves and mountain passes – treated with the wit and double meanings for which the poet is known. 196 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Author: Hồ Xuân Hương
訓huấn女nữ演diễn歌ca Huấn Nữ Diễn Ca
A teaching poem in lục bát “for the instruction of girls” (huấn nữ diễn ca), 184 lines. It sets out how a woman should live: calm and gentle in speech and manner, respectful to her parents and stepmother, in harmony with siblings, diligent and thrifty, skilled in weaving and sewing; then in marriage obedient to her husband and kind to his family, restraining him when he strays; then the care of a pregnant woman (what to eat, avoid and hear), the upbringing of children, and the choice of a son-in-law and daughter-in-law. It closes with the praise of the chaste and compliant woman. The ideals are those of the old Confucian “three obediences and four virtues”. 184 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Date: 1946-1956
𥙩lấy𫯳chồng終chung Lấy Chồng Chung
Sharing a Husband · A bitter poem of eight lines about being a co-wife. While one wife lies warm under the quilt, the other lies in the cold; the husband comes “twice a month, if at all”, and the effort she puts in is like work for hire without pay. She ends by thinking that, had she known, she would have stayed single. 8 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Author: Hồ Xuân Hương
劉lưu平bình楊dương禮lễ新tân傳truyện Lưu Bình Dương Lễ Tân Truyện A long verse retelling of the well-known story of the scholar friends Lưu Bình and Dương Lễ. The text follows the two friends from poverty and trials to success in the examinations and honours, and so tells of friendship, faithfulness and a wife who stands by her husband. It is transcribed in 15 pages. 582 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Date: 1919
美Mỹ女nữ貢cống胡hồ Mỹ Nữ Cống Hồ
Story of a beautiful woman · “Mỹ nữ cống Hồ” (A beauty given as tribute to the Hồ), a lament in song thất lục bát (189 lines, seven pages), printed in 1921 by Phúc An Đường, Hanoi. A beautiful, gifted woman speaks of her talents (music, poetry, chess, painting), compares herself with Xi Shi and with Wang Zhaojun, and blames the blind king, the flatterers at court and the Old Man of the Moon for her fate: she is handed over to the barbarian king to buy peace (“hòa Phiên”). She mourns her lonely, loveless life in the palace, recalls her parents and homeland, and ends by accepting her lot. Parts of the text are damaged or unclear. 189 lines transcribed by trung
阮nguyễn達đạt阮nguyễn生sinh新tân傳truyện Nguyễn Đạt Nguyễn Sinh Tân Truyện
A long verse tale of two poor brothers from Bình Lục, Nguyễn Đạt and Nguyễn Sinh, who leave home to study for the examinations. Sinh, burly and plain of face, is mocked by the daughter of a judge’s house, Ngọc Thanh, and ends up before the judge himself, where the servants jeer and he eats his bowl of rice unabashed; instead of driving him out the judge sets him a poem on the moon, finds a worthy man in him and keeps him in the house to study. Letters pass between the brothers, both take the prize at Sơn Nam and then pass as đồng tiến sĩ, and the judge gives Ngọc Thanh to Sinh – the tale closing on the lesson that good meets good and that the merit of the forebears brings worthy children. 251 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002
人nhân問vấn月nguyệt答đáp Nhân Vấn Nguyệt Đáp
Dialogue with the Moon · A poem in the form of a dialogue with the moon. On a clear spring night in an inn the poet asks “Aunt Moon” how old she is, why she is so carefree and how far her palace is; the moon answers that she shines for everyone and tells the good from the bad. The poet ends with the wish to reach the moon and marry Hằng Nga. 60 lines transcribed by trung
桂quế山sơn三tam元nguyên詩thi集tập：改cải良lương賦phú Quế Sơn Tam Nguyên Thi Tập Cải Lương Phú
Vietnamese Folk Theatre · A rhymed prose piece (phú) on “reform” (cải lương), in the collection of poems “Quế Sơn tam nguyên thi tập”. It is written in a lively, spoken style and strings together dozens of proverbs. The author calls his “brothers” to think and to work in a changing world: to take up farming, trade and the crafts, to learn, to stop idleness, empty show and the pursuit of rank, to change old customs without following new fashions blindly, to keep faith with friends, parents and ancestors, and to work together for the country, since all Vietnamese are of one family. 153 lines transcribed by trungAuthor: Nghè Liên Bạt
國Quốc音âm詩thi集tập Quốc Âm Thi Tập
A collection of poems in Nôm attributed to Nguyễn Trãi (Quốc âm thi tập, written around 1440), here in the printing of 1868. The twenty poems transcribed (eight lines each) sing of a simple life in a hut or hermitage among bamboo, plum and pine, of books, tea and wine, of the seasons and the moon; of loyalty and filial duty owed to prince and parents; and of a distrust of the dangers and the vanity of office. They are full of allusions to Chinese poets and sages; some lines are damaged or unclear. 160 lines transcribed by trungAuthor: Nguyễn TrãiDate: 1868, originally ca. 1440
詩thơ陳trần濟tế昌xương Thơ Trần Tế Xương
Nine short poems by Trần Tế Xương (Tú Xương) on spring, each a quatrain of four lines: four poems of feelings on a rainy spring night, with the longing for a far-away friend; two sets of New Year wishes in which the poet’s wishes turn into sharp remarks on his time (that people should at last behave like human beings); an invitation to the sisters to enjoy the spring; a night of reading by the lamp; and a poem on picking flowers for the study. 36 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Author: Trần Tế Xương
𤿰trống軍quân新tân傳truyện Trống Quân Tân Truyện
Antiphon on Literature, AB. 31 · A song in the antiphonal folk style of trống quân, with the voice of a suitor (“anh”). To a girl he offers one popular tale after another — Lý Công, Tống Trân and Cúc Hoa, the Tale of Kiều, the Butterfly and the Flower, the Twenty-Four Filial Exemplars and many more, “thirty-six tales” in all — each introduced by “or shall we hear the story of…?”. Most tales are about love and marriage, so the list is also a playful way of courting: it ends with him telling her the whole matter from first to last. The last line is cut off. 79 lines transcribed by trungDate: ca. 1946-1956
