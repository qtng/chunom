A moral poem of 1921 in the song thất lục bát metre, 108 lines long, spoken by a woman to both women and men. She mocks the educated men who waste their days on cards, dice and character riddles while the world races for rank and riches. Her target is gambling, which she sees as the ruin of the household, and she portrays the gambler with sharp irony. 108 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Date: 1921

A teaching poem in lục bát “for the instruction of girls” (huấn nữ diễn ca), 184 lines. Sharing a Husband · A bitter poem of eight lines about being a co-wife. While one wife lies warm under the quilt, the other lies in the cold; the husband comes “twice a month, if at all”, and the effort she puts in is like work for hire without pay. She ends by thinking that, had she known, she would have stayed single. 8 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Author: Hồ Xuân Hương
劉lưu平bình楊dương禮lễ新tân傳truyện Lưu Bình Dương Lễ Tân Truyện
A long verse retelling, in 582 lines on 15 pages, of the well-known story of the scholar friends Lưu Bình and Dương Lễ. Dương passes the examination and rises, Lưu fails and falls into poverty; when Lưu comes to him for help, Dương coldly turns him away, but only to spur him on. The tale praises friendship, faithfulness and a wife who stands by her husband. 582 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Date: 1919

Dialogue with the Moon · A poem in the form of a dialogue with the moon. On a clear spring night in an inn the poet asks “Aunt Moon” how old she is, why she is so carefree and how far her palace is; the moon answers that she shines for everyone and tells the good from the bad. The poet ends with the wish to reach the moon and marry Hằng Nga. 60 lines transcribed by trung

Nine short poems by Trần Tế Xương (Tú Xương) on spring, each a quatrain of four lines: four poems of feelings on a rainy spring night, with the longing for a far-away friend; two sets of New Year wishes in which the poet’s wishes turn into sharp remarks on his time (that people should at last behave like human beings); an invitation to the sisters to enjoy the spring; a night of reading by the lamp; and a poem on picking flowers for the study. 36 lines transcribed by phankhanhtoan2002Author: Trần Tế Xương
𤿰trống軍quân新tân傳truyện Trống Quân Tân Truyện
Antiphon on Literature, AB. 31 · A song in the antiphonal folk style of trống quân, with the voice of a suitor (“anh”). To a girl he offers one popular tale after another — Lý Công, Tống Trân and Cúc Hoa, the Tale of Kiều, the Butterfly and the Flower, the Twenty-Four Filial Exemplars and many more, “thirty-six tales” in all — each introduced by “or shall we hear the story of…?”. Most tales are about love and marriage, so the list is also a playful way of courting: it ends with him telling her the whole matter from first to last. The last line is cut off. 79 lines transcribed by trungDate: ca. 1946-1956
