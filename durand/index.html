His work

Maurice Durand spent his life opening the old literature of Vietnam to readers. Born in Hanoi to a French father and a Vietnamese mother, he grew up between both worlds and made their bridge his work: as a scholar of the École française d’Extrême-Orient, as a teacher in Paris, and above all in his notebooks.

Page after page they hold tales in verse, songs of ca trù, histories and poems in Chữ Nôm, the script in which Vietnamese was written for centuries, always in the same form: the characters in black brush on squared paper, and under every one its reading in red Quốc ngữ. A text that few could still read became one that anyone who reads Vietnamese can follow, character by character.

We are bringing his notebooks onto the screen, one at a time. The first is Chúa Thao cổ truyện, the old tale of Lord Thao, a young prince of the Mạc taken prisoner at Cao Bằng, in some 480 verses of lục bát. Every character is cut from the scan of the library, Durand’s reading stands below it, and every couplet has a translation.

His life

  • 1914Born in Hanoi; his father is an interpreter at the court of justice, his mother comes from Kiến An.
  • WarAn officer in Cameroon and Chad during the Second World War.
  • 1946Back in Vietnam; teaches at the École française d’Extrême-Orient and later directs it.
  • ParisTeaches Vietnamese at the École pratique des hautes études.
  • 1966Dies in Paris. His library and notebooks, and those of his father, go to Yale University.
