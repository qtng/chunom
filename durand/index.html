Nôm texts written out by hand, the reading in red under every character: the legacy of Maurice Durand, sixty years after his death.

HANOI
1914

Between two worlds

Maurice Durand was born in Hanoi in 1914, the son of an interpreter at the court of justice and of a woman from Kiến An. He grew up between French and Vietnamese, between the boulevards and the narrow streets of the old quarter, where the shop signs were still written in characters.

HANOI
1946

The scholar

After the war, which he spent as an officer in Cameroon and Chad, he came back to Hanoi and joined the École française d’Extrême-Orient. He taught there and later led it. Old Vietnamese literature became his subject: the tales in verse, the songs, the poems that had been written in Chữ Nôm for centuries and that fewer and fewer people could still read.

AT HIS DESK

His work

So he wrote them out. Notebook after notebook, always in the same form: the characters in black brush on squared paper, and under every one its reading in red Quốc ngữ. A text that few could still read became one that anyone who reads Vietnamese can follow, character by character.

PARIS
1966

His books

In Paris he taught Vietnamese at the École pratique des hautes études. He died there in 1966, at fifty-one; two of his books appeared after his death. His library and his notebooks, with the papers of his father, went to Yale University.

  • Connaissance du Viêt-Nam, with Pierre Huard, 1954
  • Phan Trần, roman en vers: text, translation and notes, 1962
  • L’Œuvre de la poétesse vietnamienne Hồ Xuân Hương, 1968: the book that brought her to Western readers; her poems from his papers are in the Bookshelf
  • Introduction à la littérature vietnamienne, with Nguyễn Trần Huân, 1969

Read today

Sixty years later his notebooks can be read on chunom.org: the first in the reader, character by character beside the scan, and ten more texts from his papers in the Bookshelf, transcribed by readers of this site over the years.
