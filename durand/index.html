Hanoi

Between two worlds

Maurice Durand was born in Hanoi in 1914. His father was an interpreter at the court of justice, his mother came from Kiến An. He grew up between French and Vietnamese, between the boulevards and the streets of the old quarter, and made the bridge between them his life’s work.

These colour photographs were taken in Hanoi in the summer of 1915. He was one year old.

The scholar

The École française d’Extrême-Orient

After the war, in which he served as an officer in Cameroon and Chad, he came back to Hanoi in 1946 and joined the École française d’Extrême-Orient, the institute that studied the history, the languages and the literature of the region. He taught there and later led it; in Paris he taught Vietnamese at the École pratique des hautes études.

At his desk

His work

His notebooks hold tales in verse, songs of ca trù, histories and poems in Chữ Nôm, the script in which Vietnamese was written for centuries, always in the same form: the characters in black brush on squared paper, and under every one its reading in red Quốc ngữ.

A text that few could still read became one that anyone who reads Vietnamese can follow, character by character.

Today

Read with us

主滔古傳

After his death in Paris in 1966 his library and notebooks, and those of his father, went to Yale University. We are bringing them onto the screen, one at a time. The first is Chúa Thao cổ truyện, the old tale of Lord Thao, a young prince of the Mạc taken prisoner at Cao Bằng, in some 480 verses of lục bát. Every character is cut from the scan of the library, Durand’s reading stands below it, and every couplet has a translation.
