Draft. Version 0.1. The specification defines the character repertoire, its code points and how a font composes the glyphs. The code points are provisional and lie in the Supplementary Private Use Area-A. There is no reference font yet, so the tables of the specification show no glyphs.

Syllable grammar

A syllable is written with the four kinds of component of a Vietnamese syllable: onset (A), nucleus (N), coda (C) and tone (T). The nucleus includes the glide: oa, uy and similar are nuclei of their own. Two control characters of PA build the cells, as in QATT: the joiner J (U+F00B0, PA JOINER) puts two parts side by side into one cell, the combiner K (U+F00B1, PA COMBINER) stacks two cells into one.

Stem     = A J N
Syllable = Stem [ T ]            one cell: the stem with a small tone mark
         | Stem C [ T ]          two cells: the stem, then the coda with its tone
         | Stem K Ext            one cell: the stem above the extension
Ext      = C [ T ]  |  T
Alone    = A | N | C [ T ] | T   a cell of its own, full size
  • A stem always has an onset. A syllable without an onset uses the onset ZERO (written z in code), which has a shape of its own: z J N.
  • Without J the onset and the nucleus are two cells (each in its full size); a stem needs J.
  • A coda without a tone is valid (tai, ngang tone, which can also be written with the tone mark ngang). A tone alone is valid (an open syllable with a tone).
  • A tone belongs to the character before it if that is a stem or a coda, and after K it is part of the stack. Anywhere else (alone, behind an onset or a nucleus that stands alone) it is a cell of its own, in full size.
  • J is only valid between an onset and a nucleus, K only between a stem and an extension.

K chooses the layout, and it is optional:

  • Without K a syllable has at most two cells side by side: the stem cell (with a small tone mark, if the tone follows the stem directly) and the coda cell (C and T in one glyph, or C alone).
  • With K between stem and extension both form one cell: the stem above, the extension below, in one em square like a CJK character. This is the form for vertical text and for mixing with Chữ Nôm.

23 onsets, 22 nuclei, 10 codas and 6 tones are encoded, 61 characters, and the joiner and the combiner, 63 in all. The combined glyphs are not encoded: the font builds them from sequences (see Composition).

Composition

A font renders the sequences with glyph substitution (OpenType ccmp). The classes tell what belongs together within a cell; the joiner and the combiner build the cells.

SequenceResult
A, N, C, Tone cell each in the full size: the master drawings (23 + 22 + 10 + 6)
A J Nstem glyph, onset and nucleus reduced to fit one cell (506)
A J N Tthe stem with a small tone mark inside the cell (the tone becomes a mark without width, 6)
C Tcoda glyph with its tone (60)
A J N C [T]two cells: the stem, then the coda
A J N K E (E = C T, C or T)one cell: the stem in its stacked form above, the extension in its stacked form below (see below)
N without an onsetthe nucleus alone in its full size; for a syllable without an onset an input method writes z J N

Every cell is one em wide.

The stacked cell

With the combiner stem and extension share one em square, like a CJK character; without it they are two cells. Both forms come from the drawings of PA 1 Normal. The stacked forms are drawn for the stack, they are not squeezed copies of the separate forms, and the font has them as glyphs of their own.

Top to bottom the stacked cell holds the stem, then the tone mark, then the coda. The stem is drawn in one of three heights, which depends on what follows the combiner:

After A J N KStem formHeight of the stem
C T (coda and tone)Zsmallest, about a quarter of the cell
C alone, or T alone (not tone 2)ZZabout a third to a half of the cell
T2 alone (huyền)ZZZlargest, more than half: tone 2 is a single bar at the bottom

So the stacked cell is not a precomposed glyph for every pair (506 × 76 = 38,456 glyphs). The font substitutes the three parts separately, as the stack of QATT does it:

  • the stem becomes its upper form Z, ZZ or ZZZ (3 × 506 glyphs), chosen by a contextual rule that looks at the characters after the combiner;
  • the combiner becomes a glyph without width;
  • the extension becomes its lower form with no advance, drawn over the cell of the stem (76 glyphs: 60 coda and tone, 10 codas, 6 tones). The coda of a coda and tone pair has three drawings by tone (for tones 1 and 2, for 3, 4 and 6, for 5), as in the separate form.

Where the drawings of the ZZZ form are missing (the onsets ph, ng, t, th, tr and the glide bars), the font uses the ZZ form.

Only the large forms of onsets, nuclei and glides are master drawings. The smaller onsets (small, extra small) and the stacked heights Z, ZZ and ZZZ are derived from them by one fixed scaling for each size and each family, applied to the strokes before they are drawn, so that the stroke keeps its width. A stem is made of its parts (onset, glide, nucleus) and has no outline of its own. Codas, tones and the lower forms of the stack are drawings of PA 1 Normal; for a form it lacks the font takes the nearest drawing, and for tone 6 in the stack the unstacked tone flattened.

PA and QATT

PA is a constructed script made for this website. Its font is a file of its own, not part of the QATT fonts and not part of the big Chữ Nôm fonts:

  • Own font. The family is loaded only by pages that show PA. The glyph rules (stems, stacked cells) are those of this font alone and need no coordination with the QATT rules.
  • Code points. QATT uses U+F0000..U+F00AF, PA U+F00B0..U+F00EE, directly behind it. Nothing overlaps, so the two fonts could be merged later without any change to a text.
  • Control characters. PA has its own joiner and combiner (U+F00B0, U+F00B1) and does not use the controls of QATT (U+F009A, U+F009B), so the two scripts never combine, even in one line. They mean the same in both: side by side in one cell, stacked in one cell. The QATT tone marks (U+F009C..U+F00A3) have no meaning in PA text, and a PA tone is not a QATT tone mark.
  • Mixed text. A page with both scripts names both families; each font draws its own code points.
  • Size. The PA font has about 2,227 glyphs: 61 master cells, 506 stems, 60 coda glyphs, 6 small tone marks, 1,518 upper forms and 76 lower forms of the stacked cell. Its cell is square like that of QATT, so mixed text keeps one line height. The two control characters are two more glyphs without width.

Reading Quốc Ngữ

A syllable of Quốc Ngữ is split into onset, nucleus, coda and tone, then mapped to the names above.

  • Tone. The accent of the vowel gives the tone and is removed: sắc 1, huyền 2, hỏi 3, ngã 4, nặng 5. No accent: no tone.
  • Onset. k and q are C; gh is G; ngh is NG; d is D; đ is DD; g before i is GI (the names of the characters, PA ONSET …). No onset is ZERO.
  • Glide. qu is the onset C plus a glide nucleus (quô is read as uâ). The glide forms oa, oă, uơ, uâ, uê, oe, uy, uyê are the nuclei OA, OAW, UOW, UAA, UEE, OE, UY, UYEE.
  • Coda. o after the nucleus is the coda U, y is I.
  • Short a. a before the codas u, y, nh, ch is written with the nucleus AW (ă).

The earlier font

The earlier PA font pa.ttf of the old site has the same repertoire in the Basic Multilingual Plane private use area (U+E001..U+E272, with the tone 6 missing). Its code points convert to this block:

Open points

  1. Tone marks. PA has its own six tone characters, as it has its own joiner and combiner. Proposal: keep them separate from QATT.
  2. Unfinished drawings. Tone 6 (ngang) is not complete in the source: its stacked forms with a coda (z-_t6) are missing, the stacked tone alone is a copy of the unstacked one, and the unstacked large tone 6 is a copy of tone 5. Its list of onsets also marks x as “todo”.
  3. Combinations that Vietnamese does not use (for instance b with a glide nucleus). Proposal: the font draws all 506 stems, the input method decides what is offered.
