Phonetic annotation

PA in one hour

A phonetic script for Vietnamese, built from Hán Nôm characters you already know.

1The idea

Every Vietnamese syllable is written as one square, like a Nôm character. Its initial and its final consonant are familiar Hán characters, read by the first sound of their reading: 巴 ba is b, 木 mộc is m. The vowel is a small stroke on the right, the tone a small mark. If you know Hán Nôm, you only learn the vowel strokes and five tone marks.

2A syllable

Watch a syllable being put together: first the parts side by side, at the end stacked into one square like a Nôm character, the initial and the vowel on top, the final and the tone below.

    • One sound, one sign: c, k, q are all 工; g, gh are 几; ng, ngh are 乂.
    • A syllable without an initial (ăn, em) starts with 𠂎, the left half of the Nôm 𧘇 ấy.
    • qu is 工 with a glide: the glide is an extra horizontal bar on the vowel stroke.
    • The finals -u, -o and -i, -y are written with the vowel strokes ô and i.
    • No mark is the level tone (ngang).

    3Initials

    23 Hán characters. Under each is the reading that gives the sound; a few take their Nôm reading, as hardly any Sino-Vietnamese word starts with r or gh. Tap to hear it.

    4Vowels

    14 vowel strokes. This is the only part to learn by heart. Tap for an example.

    With a glide (oa, uy, quê …): one more horizontal bar

    5Finals

    The same characters as the initials: -p is 巴 like b. Only -nh takes 儿, a variant of 人, so that it is not mistaken for the sắc mark. -u and -i are the vowel strokes ô and i.

    6Tones

    Five marks. Without a final the mark stands below the syllable; with a final it sits in the final's block.

    The same marks with the final -m
